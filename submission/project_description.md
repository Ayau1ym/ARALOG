# Project description

## About 250 words

ARALOG asks a narrow question: can Aralkum dust help screen how dust adheres to, shades and can be cleaned off solar hardware? The project’s claim is: **Analog fitness depends on the question being asked.** A location can be useful for one engineering test and a poor stand-in for an entire planet.

The seed study compares two Aralkum samples with a Kyzylorda control. It reports Fe in aqueous extracts at 3.7 and 2.5 mg/dm³ for Aralkum versus 0.6 mg/dm³ for the control. Those are dissolved extract values, not bulk iron. A miniature panel’s output voltage changed from 0.30 V clean to 0.22 V under friction-charged Aralkum dust (three repeats reported). That is voltage, not power or efficiency; shading and electrostatic effects were not separated. The paper also reports 25.6% cleaning in a preliminary prototype run with incomplete run and mass details.

ARALOG combines these results with NASA POWER monthly 2025 radiation, temperature, humidity and wind snapshots at the three sample coordinates. A question selector compares Aralkum, published MGS-1 properties and Mars context row by row. Evidence is tagged measured, published, hypothesis or not tested. The app gives a verdict by engineering question, not a global similarity score.

The analogy is limited: Aralkum was tested in Earth air at 50% RH and 1 g, not Mars’s low-pressure CO₂ environment, UV exposure, perchlorate context or gravity. Particle size was not measured. MGS-1 is a published simulant reference; no physical MGS-1 was tested. NASA data provide context, not mineral proof. ARALOG is an independent project, not NASA-affiliated.

## About 1000 words

ARALOG explores whether a dried seabed in Kazakhstan can inform one Mars engineering question: how dust adheres to, shades and can be removed from solar hardware. Its central claim is **Analog fitness depends on the question being asked.** It does not argue that Aralkum is a full Mars analog or a Moon analog. It treats the site as a candidate screening environment for dust-on-hardware experiments.

### Why this question matters

Solar-powered surface missions depend on array output. NASA reports that InSight generated about 5,000 watt-hours per sol after landing and about 500 watt-hours per sol by spring 2022 as dust settled on its panels. Wind-driven cleaning has also affected Opportunity. These mission histories show why deposition and removal matter to exploration systems. They do not validate an Earth material as a Mars stand-in by themselves.

### What the study measured

Three samples were collected on 28 July 2026: Aralkum Central (45.398333 N, 59.795556 E), Aralkum Coastal (45.633333 N, 60.250021 E) and a Kyzylorda control (44.828889 N, 65.519722 E). The samples were air-dried for one day at 21 °C and 50% RH.

For extract chemistry, 20 g of sample was combined with 400 mL distilled water, stirred for 10 minutes, filtered and analyzed photometrically. The paper reports Fe/Cu/Mn values of 3.7/1.1/0.07 mg/dm³ for N1, 2.5/0.8/0.1 mg/dm³ for N2, and 0.6/0.4/0 for N3. The Fe comparison is 4–6 times higher in Aralkum extracts than the control extract. This is not bulk iron and cannot be compared with a published MGS-1 FeO value in weight percent. The study did not measure hematite, total mineralogy or salt species.

A S.P.A.R.K. logger and two metal electrodes 10 mm apart were used with 8 g sample at 10 Hz for 5 seconds. The two Aralkum samples were reported to show a higher electrical response than the control. Screenshot traces near 0.285 V and 0.245 V cannot be assigned to the samples, and raw logs were not supplied. The result is qualitative-comparative and is not an absolute charge measurement.

A miniature PV cell test used a horizontal cell, flashlight at 19 cm, 5 g dust rubbed with a plastic ruler for 30 seconds, room temperature 22 °C and RH 50%; three repeats were reported. Output voltage changed from 0.30 V clean to 0.22 V with charged Aralkum dust: −0.08 V, or −26.7% relative to baseline. It is not a power or efficiency result. The experiment did not separate optical shading from electrostatic effects; standard deviation is not reported.

The paper reports a 25.6% value for an electrostatic cleaning prototype. A transparent box held 5 g dust between parallel plates 2 cm apart, a teacher-supervised source of about 220 kV ran for 60 seconds, and particle movement was observed. The paper does not provide run count, raw mass readings or electrode polarity. The app labels the figure a preliminary single-prototype report, not a validated efficiency.

### What NASA data add

The atlas includes NASA POWER monthly 2025 point snapshots for all three sample coordinates: all-sky surface shortwave radiation, 2 m air temperature, 2 m relative humidity and 10 m wind speed. Each chart draws returned monthly values and links the exact source request and retrieval date. These data characterize environmental context; they do not identify surface minerals or measure panel soiling. The project also points to NASA’s InSight power report as mission context.

EMIT L2B mineral identification is relevant, but no granule and valid pixel at the sample coordinates have yet been inspected. The app makes no EMIT mineral claim. A NASA GIBS fetch helper is included, but the proposed 24 March 2020 saline dust storm scene has not been verified. No MERRA-2 dust optical depth or MOLA/TES data are bundled.

### How to read the analogy

The analogy breaker asks what property matters: panel adhesion/cleaning, salt-driven charging, humidity, wind lofting or rover mobility. It displays separate property rows and an experiment implication instead of one similarity percentage. Aralkum has literature-described fine, salt-rich seabed context, but the study did not measure sample grain size, morphology, bulk mineralogy or total iron. Published MGS-1 specifications are literature references; no physical MGS-1 sample was tested.

Differences are central to the verdict. Tests took place in Earth air at around 1013 hPa, 50% RH and 1 g. Mars has a thin CO₂ atmosphere, very low humidity, lower gravity, different UV conditions and a perchlorate context. Quartz-rich material is not basaltic MGS-1. Aralkum is a conditional screening analog for a bench question, not a substitute for a Mars chamber or mission validation.

### What would strengthen or falsify the idea

The next study should sieve samples to a common fraction below 63 μm, control loading in g/m², log humidity and collect at least ten repeats per condition. Charged and uncharged samples plus an inert-dust control would help separate shading from electrostatics. Measure voltage, current and power separately. Quantify removal by mass and electrical recovery, and report electrode polarity, gap and voltage. Salt doping, XRF/XRD mineralogy, extract conductivity and pH, a basalt or MGS-1 reference, and a rough-vacuum CO₂ chamber would test whether behavior persists.

The claim would be weakened if matched-size, matched-loading trials show no repeatable difference from control, if voltage loss is explained by optical shading alone, or if the cleaning effect does not reproduce in mass-based measurements. ARALOG is designed to make those tests visible and falsifiable.
