# Speaker notes (7 slides, English)

## 1 — InSight makes dust an engineering constraint
InSight's reported array output fell from roughly 5,000 Wh per sol after landing to about 500 Wh per sol by spring 2022. Those are NASA-reported endpoints, not an interpolated series. Aralkum gives us a place to ask a local material question about dust on solar hardware.

## 2 — One site, one question
Our claim is that analog fitness depends on the question. We test Aralkum for dust adhesion, shading and cleaning on solar hardware, not as a whole-planet replica. That narrower claim is useful and testable.

## 3 — NASA data grounds the atlas
We retrieved NASA POWER 2025 monthly radiation, temperature, humidity and wind values at the three sample coordinates. They are site context, not mineralogy. The app keeps a separate Mars mission reference and does not blend Earth and Mars maps.

## 4 — Bench results are preliminary
The extract Fe values are aqueous concentrations. The PV observation is voltage, from 0.30 to 0.22 V, not power. Three repeats were reported, but SD is not available and optical shading is not isolated from electrostatics. The 25.6% cleaning figure is reported in the paper; run count and raw masses are missing.

## 5 — The analogy breaker keeps differences visible
Choose a question and inspect property rows, evidence labels and experiment implications. There is no global similarity number. Mars pressure, humidity, gravity, UV and perchlorate context differ, and Aralkum grain size was not measured.

## 6 — A useful tool should be falsifiable
Matched particle sizes and loading, logged humidity, uncharged and inert controls, power measurements and mass-based cleaning could overturn the screening result. A basalt/MGS-1 reference and rough-vacuum CO₂ chamber would be later steps.

## 7 — Local seed, shared protocol
The measurements are a seed, not a finished analog claim. The site, data scripts and classroom protocol are designed so other schools can contribute documented measurements. ARALOG is independent and not NASA-affiliated.
